How Grading Works in Discrete Mathematics

This course uses a proficiency-based grading system for Problem Sets and Post-Lecture Practice. The goal is to emphasize learning, revision, and demonstrated understanding rather than accumulating points.

Levels

Level 3: Proficient

You have demonstrated proficiency with the material. Level 3 represents A-range work.

Level 2: Progressing

You demonstrate good understanding of the material, but the work contains errors or needs some refinement. Level 2 represents B-range work.

Level 1: Developing

The work demonstrates some understanding, but has significant gaps, errors, or incomplete reasoning.

Level 0: No submission

Nothing was submitted for evaluation.

Nothing can come of nothing.

— William Shakespeare, King Lear, Act I, Scene I

Levels are not points. A Level 2 does not mean 2 out of 3, or 67%. The levels describe the proficiency demonstrated by your work. At the end of the semester, your work in each category will be considered as a whole and assigned a category grade.

Problem Sets

Problem Sets are substantial assignments that require you to apply the ideas developed in class. They may include calculations, proofs and mathematical arguments, applications, and conceptual questions. Correct answers matter, but so do clear reasoning and an explanation of how you reached them.

Revisions

Level 2 work may be revised to demonstrate Level 3 proficiency.

  • Revise your work based on the feedback.
  • Submit the improved version within one week of receiving feedback.
  • Be prepared to explain your revisions if asked.

Level 1 work may also be revised.

  • Come to office hours or schedule a meeting with me to discuss the work.
  • Revise the assignment based on our discussion and the feedback.
  • Submit the improved version within one week of receiving feedback.

The level recorded after revision reflects the highest level of proficiency you ultimately demonstrate, not an average of your attempts.

At the end of the semester, the Problem Sets category will receive a grade based on your work as a whole. Consistent Level 3 work represents the A range; consistent Level 2 work represents the B range. A mixture of levels will be evaluated based on the overall pattern of your work, including improvement and demonstrated proficiency over time.

Post-Lecture Practice

Post-Lecture Practice consists of short sets of problems assigned after some lectures. These exercises give you a chance to work with new ideas while they are still fresh and help both of us see what may need more attention.

  • Level 3 — Proficient: sound understanding and appropriate reasoning; minor errors are acceptable.
  • Level 2 — Progressing: the main ideas are present, but the work contains errors, gaps, or incomplete reasoning.
  • Level 1 — Developing: some relevant progress is shown, but substantial understanding is still missing.
  • Level 0 — No submission.

A small computational or notational error will not by itself prevent a Level 3 if the mathematical reasoning is sound. However, a serious attempt is not automatically proficient: the work must also demonstrate an appropriate understanding of the mathematics.

Post-Lecture Practice cannot be revised or made up. Your two lowest levels will be dropped. This provides flexibility for an occasional missed assignment, technical problem, or simply having an off week.

After the two lowest levels are dropped, your remaining Post-Lecture Practice will be considered as a whole and assigned a category grade.

Research Project

The Research Project is a group project. It will be evaluated as a whole, with attention to the mathematics, the clarity and organization of the written work, and the effectiveness of the presentation. The project grade is not produced by adding up points for many small requirements.

Everyone in the group is expected to contribute meaningfully and to understand the main ideas of the project. Ordinarily, group members will receive the same grade. Individual grades may differ when there is a clear difference in contribution or demonstrated understanding.

Exams

Exams will receive conventional numerical and letter grades. Your work must include the reasoning that leads to your answer. A final answer without an explanation will be considered an unsolved problem, even if the answer is correct.

Partial credit will be based on the mathematical progress demonstrated in your work. Clear reasoning may receive substantial credit even when the final answer is incorrect.

A Note About Category Grades

Proficiency grading is intentionally different from averaging points. I am interested in the level of understanding you ultimately demonstrate.

For example, a student whose Problem Set levels develop from 1 → 2 → 2 → 3 → 3 has shown a different pattern of learning from a student whose work moves in the opposite direction. The final category grade may reflect that difference.

The purpose of revision is not to collect additional points. It is to learn the mathematics and demonstrate that you have learned it.
