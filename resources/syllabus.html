Course Description:

Prerequisites:
Precalculus or higher (grade of C or better)

Covers a variety of topics that provide a foundation for both higher mathematics and computer science. These topics may include logic and formal proofs, set theory, number theory, probability, mathematical induction, algorithms, recurrence relations, equivalence relations and partial orderings, and topics in graph theory (including isomorphism, traversability, planarity, and colorings).

Learning Outcomes

  • Analyze structures like sets, functions, relations, equivalence relations, partial orders, trees, and graphs
  • Apply counting principles using the multiplicative property, factorials, and binomial coefficients
  • Analyze and construct rigorous arguments using logic
  • Convert between numerical bases
  • Use proof techniques such as induction, contradiction, and direct proof
  • Typeset mathematical documents using LaTeX
  • Use Git and GitHub to manage and submit assignments

Problem Sets

Problem sets are substantial assignments that provide practice with the central ideas and techniques of the course. Solutions should include the reasoning that leads to an answer, not merely the answer itself. Problem sets must be typeset in LaTeX and submitted through GitHub. Discussing problems with classmates is encouraged, but all submitted solutions must be written independently and in your own words.

Post-Lecture Practice

After selected lectures, you will receive a short set of problems designed to reinforce the material introduced in class and help you identify questions before we move on. These exercises are shorter than the problem sets and address the material from the preceding lecture.

Exams

There will be two midsemester exams and a cumulative final exam, each worth 15% of the course grade. If your final-exam score is higher than your lower midsemester-exam score, the final-exam score will also replace that lower score. Otherwise, all three original exam scores will remain unchanged.

Problem-Solving Presentation

Once during the semester, each student will give a brief in-class presentation about a problem they initially found challenging but ultimately understood. The problem may come from a course problem set or another approved source. The presentation should describe the difficulty encountered, approaches attempted, the key insight, and a justified solution. Students will submit a short proposal in advance and will be assigned a presentation window. Evaluation will emphasize mathematical reasoning, reflection, correctness, and clarity rather than polished public-speaking performance.

Group Research Project

You will work with a group to explore a topic connected to discrete mathematics. At the end of the semester, your group will give a short presentation and submit a written report. We will discuss possible topics, group formation, and project expectations in class.

Collaboration Policy/Academic Integrity

Discussing homework is encouraged - copying is not. All submitted work must be your own. Plagiarizing another student’s work can lead to academic sanctions per the Purchase College’s Academic Integrity Policy.

We take cheating seriously!

Policy on ChatGPT and Large Language Models (LLMs)

  1. Use AI for exploration. You may use tools such as ChatGPT to ask questions, explore mathematical ideas and applications, request conceptual explanations, or investigate topics beyond an assignment.
  2. Do your own work. AI may not solve assigned problems or generate, rewrite, or substantially edit work that you submit. Your proofs, explanations, calculations, code, and LaTeX must represent your own mathematical reasoning.
  3. Verify your sources. AI tools can produce convincing but incorrect definitions, calculations, proofs, examples, and references. Check any information they provide against course materials or other reliable sources. AI is not itself a source.
  4. Be transparent. Disclose any substantive AI assistance connected to submitted work, including what tool you used and how you used it. You must be prepared to explain and defend every part of your submission.

Violations of this policy will be treated as academic dishonesty and plagiarism, in accordance with Purchase College’s Academic Integrity Policy.
